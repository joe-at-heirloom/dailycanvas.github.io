Famous paintings at the Met

The most famous paintings at The Metropolitan Museum of Art include Emanuel Leutze's Washington Crossing the Delaware, Vincent van Gogh's Wheat Field with Cypresses, John Singer Sargent's Madame X, Pieter Bruegel the Elder's The Harvesters and Paul Cézanne's The Card Players.

About the museum

The Met opened in 1872 and has been on Fifth Avenue beside Central Park since 1880. Its collection spans five thousand years and every continent; the European paintings galleries on the second floor, with the 19th- and early 20th-century rooms beside them, hold most of the works below.

It's too big to see in a day. For paintings alone, start with the European galleries, add the American Wing for Leutze and Sargent, and save the Asian art galleries, where Japanese prints are shown in rotation, for another visit.

Questions

What is the most famous painting at the Met?

Washington Crossing the Delaware (1851), in the American Wing, is the best-known single painting. Van Gogh's Wheat Field with Cypresses, Sargent's Madame X and Bruegel's The Harvesters are close behind.

Does the Met have a Van Gogh?

Several, including Wheat Field with Cypresses, Irises and Self-Portrait with a Straw Hat.

How long does it take to see the Met?

You can't see it all in a day. Two or three hours covers the European paintings and the American Wing.
