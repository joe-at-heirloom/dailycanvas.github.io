Realism: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Realism is the movement that began in France around 1848, when painters such as Gustave Courbet and Jean-François Millet turned from myth and history to paint ordinary people and everyday life as they really were.

History

Realism emerged in France around 1848, the year of revolutions across Europe. Its painters rejected the idealized history, myth and exotic drama of the Academy and the Romantics to paint ordinary people and modern life truthfully.

Gustave Courbet was its leader. His A Burial at Ornans (1849–50) gave a village funeral the scale of a history painting, and in 1855, when works were refused by the Exposition Universelle, he showed them in his own Pavilion of Realism. Jean-François Millet painted peasants at work, Honoré Daumier satirized the city, and Rosa Bonheur painted animals with scientific accuracy.

The Barbizon painters, including Camille Corot and Théodore Rousseau, brought the same honesty to landscape. Realism spread across Europe and to America, where Winslow Homer and Thomas Eakins became its great figures, and it opened the way to the modern-life subjects of Manet and the Impressionists.

How to recognize it

Peasants, workers and everyday life at full scale; earthy colors; no idealizing or moralizing gloss.

Key artists: Gustave Courbet, Jean-François Millet, Honoré Daumier, Rosa Bonheur, Winslow Homer.

Questions

What is Realism in art?

A movement that began in France around 1848, whose painters showed ordinary people and everyday life truthfully, without idealizing them or drawing on myth and history.

Who are the most famous Realist painters?

Gustave Courbet, Jean-François Millet, Honoré Daumier, Rosa Bonheur and Camille Corot in France, and Winslow Homer and Thomas Eakins in the United States.

What is the most famous Realist painting?

Jean-François Millet's The Gleaners (1857) and Gustave Courbet's A Burial at Ornans (1849–50), both at the Musée d'Orsay in Paris.
