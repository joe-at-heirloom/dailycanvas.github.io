Post-Impressionism: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Post-Impressionism names the painters who, from the mid-1880s to about 1905, took Impressionism's bright color in personal directions: Paul Cézanne toward structure, Vincent van Gogh toward emotion, Paul Gauguin toward symbol, and Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec toward the nightlife of Montmartre.

History

Post-Impressionism names the painters who, from the mid-1880s, took Impressionism's bright color and modern subjects in personal directions. The English critic Roger Fry coined the term for his 1910 exhibition in London, Manet and the Post-Impressionists.

Paul Cézanne rebuilt nature from planes of color and simple forms. Vincent van Gogh used color and thick, rhythmic brushwork to express emotion. Paul Gauguin flattened color and outline into symbols, first in Brittany, then in Tahiti. Georges Seurat built pictures from dots of color, and Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec drew the cabarets and dance halls of Montmartre.

They never saw themselves as a group, but together they opened the way to the 20th century: Cézanne to Cubism, Van Gogh to Expressionism, and Gauguin to the Fauves and the Symbolists.

How to recognize it

Bold, expressive color; thick or patterned brushwork; simplified, solid forms; personal vision over optical accuracy.

Key artists: Paul Cézanne, Vincent van Gogh, Paul Gauguin, Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec.

Questions

What is Post-Impressionism?

Not one style but a generation of painters, active from about 1886 to 1905, who went beyond Impressionism's record of light toward structure, emotion and symbol.

Who are the Post-Impressionist painters?

Paul Cézanne, Vincent van Gogh, Paul Gauguin, Georges Seurat and Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec are the major figures.

Who coined the term Post-Impressionism?

The English critic Roger Fry, for his exhibition Manet and the Post-Impressionists at the Grafton Galleries in London in 1910.
