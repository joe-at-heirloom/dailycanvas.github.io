The Northern Renaissance: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

The Northern Renaissance is the art of Flanders, the Netherlands and Germany from about 1430 to 1580, when painters such as Jan van Eyck, Hieronymus Bosch, Albrecht Dürer and Pieter Bruegel the Elder used oil paint to capture light, texture and detail as never before.

History

In the 1420s and 1430s, painters in Flanders, then ruled by the Dukes of Burgundy, perfected painting in layered oil glazes, which let them render light, texture and detail with new precision. Jan van Eyck's Ghent Altarpiece, completed in 1432, and his Arnolfini Portrait of 1434 are its founding masterpieces.

Rogier van der Weyden, Hans Memling and Gerard David carried the style through the century, and Hieronymus Bosch filled triptychs with fantastic visions. In Germany, Albrecht Dürer traveled to Italy and brought Renaissance ideas of proportion and perspective home, while Lucas Cranach the Elder and Hans Holbein the Younger painted the age of the Reformation.

In the mid-16th century Pieter Bruegel the Elder turned to peasants, proverbs and the seasons, opening the way to the everyday subjects of Dutch painting in the next century.

How to recognize it

Jewel-like detail and texture; deep, glowing color from layered oil glazes; everyday objects charged with meaning.

Key artists: Jan van Eyck, Rogier van der Weyden, Hieronymus Bosch, Albrecht Dürer, Hans Holbein the Younger, Pieter Bruegel the Elder.

Questions

What is the difference between the Italian and the Northern Renaissance?

Italian painters built on ancient Greek and Roman art, idealized bodies and mathematical perspective, often in fresco. Northern painters, working mostly in oil on wood panels, excelled at minute, real-looking detail, textures and hidden symbolism.

Who are the most important Northern Renaissance painters?

Jan van Eyck, Rogier van der Weyden, Hans Memling, Hieronymus Bosch, Albrecht Dürer, Lucas Cranach the Elder, Hans Holbein the Younger and Pieter Bruegel the Elder.

What is the most famous Northern Renaissance painting?

Jan van Eyck's Arnolfini Portrait (1434), in the National Gallery in London, and Hieronymus Bosch's The Garden of Earthly Delights, in the Prado.
