Symbolism: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Symbolism is the late 19th-century movement whose painters, among them Gustave Moreau, Odilon Redon, Arnold Böcklin and Edvard Munch, turned from the visible world to dreams, myth, death and the inner life.

History

Symbolism was a movement in literature and art of the 1880s and 1890s. The poet Jean Moréas published its manifesto in Paris in 1886, but painters such as Gustave Moreau and Pierre Puvis de Chavannes had already turned from the visible world to myth, dream and the inner life.

Odilon Redon drew floating eyes and strange creatures in charcoal before turning, in the 1890s, to radiant flowers and visions in color. In Switzerland Arnold Böcklin painted Island of the Dead, in Belgium Fernand Khnopff painted silent, mysterious figures, and in Norway Edvard Munch painted love, anxiety and death.

Symbolist themes, among them femmes fatales, sphinxes, death, silence and longing, ran through Art Nouveau and the Vienna Secession, and the movement's turn inward prepared the way for Expressionism and Surrealism.

How to recognize it

Mysterious, often eerie scenes; femmes fatales and sphinxes; twilight and silence.

Key artists: Gustave Moreau, Odilon Redon, Arnold Böcklin, Fernand Khnopff, Edvard Munch.

Questions

What is Symbolism in art?

A late 19th-century movement whose artists painted dreams, myths, moods and ideas rather than the visible world, using images as symbols.

Who are the main Symbolist painters?

Gustave Moreau, Odilon Redon, Pierre Puvis de Chavannes, Arnold Böcklin, Fernand Khnopff and Edvard Munch.

Is The Scream a Symbolist painting?

Yes. Edvard Munch painted it in 1893, at the height of Symbolism, and it is also seen as a founding work of Expressionism.
