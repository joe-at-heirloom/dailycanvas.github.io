Romanticism: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Romanticism is the movement of about 1790 to 1850 that put feeling, imagination and the overwhelming power of nature ahead of reason. Its painters include Caspar David Friedrich, J. M. W. Turner, John Constable, Francisco Goya, Théodore Géricault and Eugène Delacroix.

History

Romanticism began in the late 18th century as a reaction against the reason and order of the Enlightenment and against the Industrial Revolution. Romantic artists valued feeling, imagination and the individual, and the overwhelming power of nature, which they called the sublime.

In Germany, Caspar David Friedrich painted lone figures before mountains, seas and ruins. In England, J. M. W. Turner turned storms and sunlight into almost abstract color, and John Constable painted the English countryside from close observation. In Spain, Francisco Goya painted the horrors of war and his own nightmares.

In France, Théodore Géricault's The Raft of the Medusa (1818–19) and Eugène Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People (1830) brought shipwreck and revolution to vast canvases, in open rivalry with the Neoclassical line of Ingres. By the 1840s and 1850s, Realism was taking its place.

How to recognize it

Dramatic skies and light; loose, energetic brushwork; the sublime in nature; heroism, terror and longing.

Key artists: Francisco Goya, Caspar David Friedrich, J. M. W. Turner, John Constable, Théodore Géricault, Eugène Delacroix.

Questions

What are the characteristics of Romantic art?

Strong emotion, imagination and the sublime power of nature, painted with dramatic light and loose, expressive brushwork, from storms and shipwrecks to revolutions and solitary wanderers.

Who are the most famous Romantic painters?

Caspar David Friedrich, J. M. W. Turner, John Constable, Francisco Goya, Théodore Géricault and Eugène Delacroix.

What is the most famous Romantic painting?

Caspar David Friedrich's Wanderer above the Sea of Fog (c. 1818) is its emblem, with Géricault's The Raft of the Medusa and Delacroix's Liberty Leading the People.
