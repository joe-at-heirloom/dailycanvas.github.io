The Hudson River School: the paintings, the painters and the history

The Hudson River School was America's first homegrown school of painting: from about 1825 to 1875, Thomas Cole, Frederic Edwin Church, Albert Bierstadt and others painted the Hudson Valley, the Catskills and later the American West as grand, glowing wilderness.

History

The Hudson River School began in 1825, when Thomas Cole sailed up the Hudson to sketch and brought back landscapes that sold at once in New York.

Cole and Asher B. Durand made the wilderness of the Catskills, the Adirondacks and the White Mountains a national subject, painted with a reverence that was part religious, part patriotic. A second generation traveled farther: Frederic Edwin Church to South America and the Arctic, Albert Bierstadt and Thomas Moran to the Rockies, Yosemite and Yellowstone.

Some of them, including Sanford Robinson Gifford, John Frederick Kensett, Fitz Henry Lane and Martin Johnson Heade, became known for calm, glowing light, later called Luminism. After the Civil War, taste turned toward French art, and the name Hudson River School was first used, dismissively, in the 1870s.

How to recognize it

Panoramic views; glowing, golden light; tiny human figures dwarfed by mountains, falls and sky.

Key artists: Thomas Cole, Asher B. Durand, Frederic Edwin Church, Albert Bierstadt.

Questions

What is the Hudson River School?

A group of American landscape painters active from about 1825 to 1875, led by Thomas Cole, who painted the Hudson Valley, the Catskills and later the American West as grand, luminous wilderness.

Who are the Hudson River School painters?

Thomas Cole, Asher B. Durand, Frederic Edwin Church, Albert Bierstadt, Thomas Moran, Sanford Robinson Gifford, John Frederick Kensett, Jasper Francis Cropsey and Robert S. Duncanson.

Where can I see Hudson River School paintings?

The Met, the New-York Historical Society, the National Gallery of Art, the Smithsonian American Art Museum and the Wadsworth Atheneum in Hartford, and in the Hudson Valley at the Thomas Cole National Historic Site and Olana, Frederic Church's home.
