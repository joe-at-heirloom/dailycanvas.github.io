Impressionism: the paintings, the painters and how to recognize it

Impressionism is the movement of French painters, led by Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Edgar Degas, Camille Pissarro and Berthe Morisot, who from the 1860s painted quickly, often outdoors, in visible strokes of bright color to capture light and modern life.

History

Impressionism began in Paris in the 1860s among young painters who met in Charles Gleyre's studio and the cafés of the Batignolles: Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Alfred Sisley and Frédéric Bazille, joined by Camille Pissarro, Edgar Degas, Paul Cézanne and Berthe Morisot. The official Salon kept rejecting their work.

In 1874 they held their own exhibition in the former studio of the photographer Nadar. The critic Louis Leroy mocked Monet's Impression, Sunrise and called them all Impressionists, and they took the name. Seven more exhibitions followed, the last in 1886.

They painted outdoors and quickly, in broken strokes of pure color, to catch light and atmosphere as they changed, and they painted modern life: railways, cafés, boating parties and boulevards. The dealer Paul Durand-Ruel championed them, and his 1886 exhibition in New York found eager American buyers.

How to recognize it

Short, visible brushstrokes; bright, unblended color; colored shadows; everyday modern subjects and open-air light.

Key artists: Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Edgar Degas, Camille Pissarro, Berthe Morisot, Alfred Sisley, Mary Cassatt.

Questions

What is Impressionism?

A movement in French painting of the 1860s to 1880s whose artists painted quickly, often outdoors, in visible strokes of bright color to capture light, atmosphere and modern life.

Who are the most famous Impressionist painters?

Claude Monet, Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Edgar Degas, Camille Pissarro, Berthe Morisot, Alfred Sisley, Mary Cassatt and Gustave Caillebotte, with Édouard Manet as their forerunner.

How did Impressionism get its name?

From Monet's Impression, Sunrise (1872). After the first exhibition in 1874 the critic Louis Leroy used its title to mock the whole group, and they adopted the name.
