Neoclassicism: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Neoclassicism is the art of about 1760 to 1830 that looked back to ancient Greece and Rome for clear outlines, calm composition and stories of virtue. Its leading painters were Jacques-Louis David and Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres.

Dates
c. 1760–1830
Where
France, then Europe and America
Before
Rococo
After
Romanticism

History

Neoclassicism grew in the 1750s and 1760s out of excitement about the excavations of Herculaneum and Pompeii and the writings of the German scholar Johann Joachim Winckelmann, who praised the "noble simplicity and quiet grandeur" of Greek art.

In Rome, Anton Raphael Mengs, Pompeo Batoni and Angelica Kauffman painted in the new style, and the American-born Benjamin West took it to London. In Paris, Jacques-Louis David made it the art of civic virtue with Oath of the Horatii (1784) and, after the Revolution, the art of Napoleon's empire.

David's pupils carried it into the 19th century, above all Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres, whose flawless line set the standard of the French Academy against the color and passion of Romantics such as Eugène Delacroix.

How to recognize it

Crisp outlines and smooth, invisible brushwork; calm, frieze-like compositions; Roman architecture and costume.

Key artists: Jacques-Louis David, Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres, Angelica Kauffman, Antonio Canova.

Questions

What is Neoclassical art?

Art of about 1760 to 1830 inspired by ancient Greece and Rome: clear outlines, calm compositions, smooth surfaces and stories of courage and sacrifice.

Who are the main Neoclassical painters?

Jacques-Louis David, Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres, Angelica Kauffman, Anton Raphael Mengs and Benjamin West.

What is the difference between Neoclassicism and Romanticism?

Neoclassicism prizes reason, order and clear line. Romanticism, which grew up beside it and outlasted it, prizes feeling, imagination and expressive color.
