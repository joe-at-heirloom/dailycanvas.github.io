Rococo art: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Rococo is the light, playful style of the 18th century that began in Paris around 1720: pastel colors, curving forms and scenes of love and leisure, painted by Antoine Watteau, François Boucher, Jean-Honoré Fragonard and, in Venice, Giovanni Battista Tiepolo.

History

Rococo took hold in Paris after the death of Louis XIV in 1715, when the aristocracy left the formal court at Versailles for elegant town houses and wanted lighter art to match. The name comes from rocaille, the shell and pebble work of garden grottoes.

Antoine Watteau invented the fête galante, scenes of elegant lovers in dreamy parks. François Boucher, a favorite of Madame de Pompadour, painted pastel mythologies and boudoir scenes, and Jean-Honoré Fragonard's The Swing (c. 1767) is the style at its most playful. Portraitists such as Jean-Marc Nattier and Élisabeth Vigée Le Brun painted the court.

In Venice, Giovanni Battista Tiepolo painted vast, airy ceilings, and Canaletto and Francesco Guardi painted views of the city for travelers on the Grand Tour. By the 1760s and 1770s critics attacked Rococo as frivolous, and Neoclassicism replaced it.

How to recognize it

Pastel colors; curving, decorative forms; feathery brushwork; aristocrats at play.

Key artists: Antoine Watteau, François Boucher, Jean-Honoré Fragonard, Giovanni Battista Tiepolo.

Questions

What is Rococo art?

A light, decorative style of about 1720 to 1780, born in France, with pastel colors, curving forms and playful scenes of love and leisure.

Who are the main Rococo painters?

Antoine Watteau, François Boucher and Jean-Honoré Fragonard in France, and Giovanni Battista Tiepolo in Italy.

Where does the word rococo come from?

From the French rocaille, the shell and pebble work of grottoes, probably blended with barocco. It began as a mocking term.
