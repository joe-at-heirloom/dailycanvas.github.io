Baroque art: the paintings, the artists and how to recognize it

Baroque art is the dramatic, emotional style that began in Rome around 1600 and spread across Catholic Europe until about 1750. Its painters, from Caravaggio and Rubens to Velázquez, used strong light and shadow, movement and theatrical moments to move the viewer.

History

Baroque art began in Rome around 1600, when Caravaggio's dramatic realism and Annibale Carracci's revival of classical grandeur offered two answers to the stylized art of Mannerism. The Catholic Church, renewing itself after the Reformation, wanted art that would move ordinary believers, and Baroque painters gave it emotion, movement and theatrical light.

The style spread across Catholic Europe. Peter Paul Rubens in Antwerp filled churches and palaces with swirling, sensual compositions. In Spain, Diego Velázquez painted the court of Philip IV, and Francisco de Zurbarán and Bartolomé Esteban Murillo painted for monasteries and churches. In France, Nicolas Poussin and Claude Lorrain created a calmer, classical Baroque.

Caravaggio's followers, the Caravaggisti, including Artemisia Gentileschi, carried his light and shadow from Naples to Utrecht. In the Protestant Dutch Republic, painting took a different, domestic turn, covered in the movements guide as the Dutch Golden Age.

How to recognize it

Chiaroscuro, sharp light against deep shadow; diagonal, swirling compositions; rich color and theatrical gesture.

Key artists: Caravaggio, Artemisia Gentileschi, Peter Paul Rubens, Gian Lorenzo Bernini, Diego Velázquez.

Questions

What are the characteristics of Baroque art?

Drama and emotion: strong contrasts of light and dark, diagonal compositions, rich color, movement, and scenes caught at their most intense moment.

Who are the most famous Baroque painters?

Caravaggio, Peter Paul Rubens, Diego Velázquez, Rembrandt, Artemisia Gentileschi, Anthony van Dyck and Nicolas Poussin.

What is the difference between Renaissance and Baroque art?

Renaissance art aims for balance, harmony and calm. Baroque art aims for drama, movement and emotion, with stronger light and deeper shadow.
