Johannes Vermeer: paintings, life and where to see them

Johannes Vermeer (1632–1675) was a Dutch painter of quiet interiors filled with light. Only about 35 of his paintings survive, and they include Girl with a Pearl Earring and The Milkmaid, two of the most loved pictures in the world.

Life

Vermeer spent his whole life in Delft, where his father was an innkeeper and art dealer. He married Catharina Bolnes in 1653, joined the painters' guild that year, and was elected one of its heads several times.

He worked slowly, perhaps two or three paintings a year, often of a woman alone by a window: reading, writing, pouring milk or weighing pearls. He used costly pigments, including ultramarine ground from lapis lazuli, and his exact methods, perhaps including a camera obscura, are still debated.

The French invasion of 1672 wrecked the Dutch art market, and Vermeer died in debt in 1675, at 43, leaving a large family. He was largely forgotten until the French critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger championed him in the 1860s.

Where to see Vermeer's work

The Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam has four, including The Milkmaid, and the Mauritshuis in The Hague has three, including Girl with a Pearl Earring and View of Delft. In the US, the Met has five, the Frick Collection three and the National Gallery of Art two.

Questions

How many paintings did Vermeer make?

About 34 to 37 paintings are attributed to him, depending on the scholar. He worked slowly and died at 43.

What is Vermeer's most famous painting?

Girl with a Pearl Earring (c. 1665), in the Mauritshuis in The Hague. The Milkmaid, in the Rijksmuseum, is a close second.

Was a Vermeer ever stolen?

Yes. The Concert was stolen from the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston in March 1990, with twelve other works, and has never been recovered.
