J. M. W. Turner: paintings, life and where to see them

J. M. W. Turner (1775–1851) was an English Romantic painter of light, weather and the sea, and the most famous of British artists. His late, almost abstract storms and sunsets anticipated Impressionism by decades.

Life

The son of a London barber, Turner entered the Royal Academy Schools at 14 and showed his first watercolor at the Academy a year later. He traveled Britain making topographical watercolors and became a full Royal Academician in 1802, at 26.

Inspired by Claude Lorrain, he painted grand historical landscapes, then storms, fires and shipwrecks in which form dissolves into light. Venice, which he first visited in 1819, became a lifelong subject.

His late works, The Fighting Temeraire (1839), The Slave Ship (1840) and Rain, Steam and Speed (1844), puzzled many critics. He left about 300 oil paintings and 30,000 works on paper to the nation, now mostly at Tate Britain, and Britain's best-known contemporary art award is named after him.

Where to see Turner's work

Tate Britain's Clore Gallery holds the Turner Bequest, and the National Gallery in London has The Fighting Temeraire and Rain, Steam and Speed. In the US, the National Gallery of Art, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston (The Slave Ship), the Yale Center for British Art, the Frick Collection and the Cleveland Museum of Art have important paintings.

Questions

What is Turner's most famous painting?

The Fighting Temeraire (1839), at the National Gallery in London, voted Britain's favorite painting in a 2005 BBC Radio 4 poll.

What is the Turner Prize?

An annual award for contemporary art in Britain, founded in 1984 and named after Turner, who had hoped to found a prize for young landscape painters.

Where can I see Turner's paintings?

Tate Britain in London holds the Turner Bequest of about 300 oil paintings and 30,000 works on paper. In the US, the National Gallery of Art in Washington has one of the best groups of his oils.
