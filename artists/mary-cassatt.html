Mary Cassatt: paintings, life and where to see them

Mary Cassatt (1844–1926) was an American painter and printmaker who lived in Paris and was the only American to exhibit with the Impressionists. She is best known for tender, unsentimental pictures of women and children.

Life

Cassatt was born near Pittsburgh into a well-off family, studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts, and moved to Paris in 1866, against her father's wishes, to study the Old Masters.

Edgar Degas saw her work at the Salon and invited her to join the Impressionists; she showed with them in 1879, 1880, 1881 and 1886. In the 1890s, inspired by Japanese prints, she made a celebrated series of color prints, and her paintings centered more and more on mothers and children.

She also advised American collectors, above all her friends Louisine and Henry Havemeyer, whose bequest brought many Impressionist paintings to the Met. Failing eyesight ended her painting around 1914, and she died in France in 1926.

Where to see Cassatt's work

The National Gallery of Art in Washington has many of her best paintings, including Little Girl in a Blue Armchair and The Boating Party. The Art Institute of Chicago has The Child's Bath, and the Met and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston have important works.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (10); Smithsonian American Art Museum, Washington, DC (3); Art Institute of Chicago (2).

Questions

What is Mary Cassatt's most famous painting?

The Child's Bath (1893), at the Art Institute of Chicago, and Little Girl in a Blue Armchair (1878), at the National Gallery of Art.

Was Mary Cassatt an Impressionist?

Yes. Degas invited her to join the group, and she showed in four of the Impressionist exhibitions, the only American to do so.

Where can I see Mary Cassatt's paintings?

The National Gallery of Art in Washington has the best group, and the Art Institute of Chicago, the Met and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston have important works.
