Paul Gauguin: paintings, life and where to see them

Paul Gauguin (1848–1903) was a French Post-Impressionist painter known for flat, glowing color and his paintings of Brittany and Tahiti. His bold simplifications influenced the Fauves and modern art, though his life in Polynesia is viewed critically today.

Life

Gauguin spent his early childhood in Lima, Peru, served in the merchant marine and the navy, and became a successful Paris stockbroker who collected Impressionist paintings. He painted on Sundays, showed with the Impressionists from 1879, and turned to art full time after the stock market crash of 1882.

In Pont-Aven in Brittany he developed a style of flat areas of color inside dark outlines, seen in Vision after the Sermon (1888). That autumn he joined Van Gogh in Arles; their nine weeks together ended in Van Gogh's breakdown.

In 1891 he left for Tahiti, looking for a world untouched by Europe, and painted his best-known pictures there, including Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going? (1897–98). His relationships there with girls as young as 13 are part of how he is judged now. He died in the Marquesas Islands in 1903.

Where to see Gauguin's work

The Musée d'Orsay has the largest group in France, and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston has Where Do We Come From? The National Gallery of Art, the Met, the Art Institute of Chicago and the Cleveland Museum of Art have strong collections in the US.

The paintings on this page: Art Institute of Chicago (8); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (7); National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (4); Cleveland Museum of Art (1).

Questions

What is Gauguin's most famous painting?

Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going? (1897–98), at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. Vision after the Sermon (1888), in the Scottish National Gallery, is the key early work.

Did Gauguin and Van Gogh live together?

Yes, for about nine weeks in the autumn of 1888, in the Yellow House in Arles. It ended in December when Van Gogh, in a crisis, cut off his ear.

Why is Gauguin controversial?

Because of how he lived in Tahiti and the Marquesas, including sexual relationships with girls as young as 13, and because his paintings fantasize about Polynesian women. Museums now present his work with that history.
