Henri Matisse: paintings, life and where to see them

Henri Matisse (1869–1954) was a French artist who, with Picasso, defined modern art. He led the Fauves, the "wild beasts" of pure color, in 1905, and in his last years invented a new art of cut paper.

Life

Matisse trained as a lawyer and only began painting in 1889, when his mother gave him a box of paints while he recovered from appendicitis. He studied in Paris under Gustave Moreau and painted in a traditional manner until about 1904.

In the summer of 1905 at Collioure he and André Derain began painting in brilliant, unmixed color, and at that autumn's Salon d'Automne a critic called them fauves. Collectors including Gertrude and Leo Stein and the Russian Sergei Shchukin bought his work, and Shchukin commissioned The Dance and Music (1910).

From 1917 he lived mostly in Nice. After surgery for cancer in 1941 left him unable to stand for long, he worked from his bed or wheelchair, cutting shapes from paper painted with gouache, as in the book Jazz (1947). His last great work was the Chapel of the Rosary in Vence (1948–51).

Where to see Matisse's work

In the US, MoMA, the Barnes Foundation in Philadelphia and the Baltimore Museum of Art (the Cone Collection) have major Matisse collections. In Europe: the Centre Pompidou in Paris, the Musée Matisse in Nice, the Hermitage in St Petersburg, and the Statens Museum for Kunst in Copenhagen, which owns Portrait of Madame Matisse (The Green Stripe).

The paintings on this page: Statens Museum for Kunst, Copenhagen (8); Art Institute of Chicago (3); State Hermitage Museum, St Petersburg (1).

Questions

What is Matisse's most famous painting?

The Dance (1910), painted for Sergei Shchukin and now in the Hermitage in St Petersburg. Woman with a Hat (1905) and his late paper cut-outs are also among his best known.

What is Fauvism?

A short-lived style of about 1905–08, led by Matisse and André Derain, that used intense, unnatural color to express feeling. The name comes from the French fauves, "wild beasts".

What are Matisse's cut-outs?

In his seventies, after major surgery, Matisse cut shapes from sheets of paper painted with gouache and had them pinned into compositions. He called it drawing with scissors.
