Berthe Morisot: paintings, life and where to see them

Berthe Morisot (1841–1895) was a French painter and a founding member of the Impressionists. She showed in seven of their eight exhibitions and painted women's lives with quick, airy brushwork.

Life

Morisot and her sister Edma trained as painters from their teens, copying in the Louvre and taking advice from Camille Corot on painting outdoors. She first showed at the Salon in 1864.

She met Édouard Manet in 1868, sat for several of his paintings and in 1874 married his brother Eugène. That year she showed at the first Impressionist exhibition, including The Cradle. She missed only the 1879 exhibition, after the birth of her daughter Julie.

Her subjects were the women and children around her, in gardens, drawing rooms and boats, painted in loose, feathery strokes. She died of pneumonia in 1895, at 54, and her friends Degas, Monet and Renoir organized a memorial exhibition the next year.

Where to see Morisot's work

The Musée Marmottan Monet in Paris has the largest collection of her work, and the Musée d'Orsay has The Cradle. In the US, the National Gallery of Art has The Harbor at Lorient and many more, and the Met and the Art Institute of Chicago have strong works.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (11); Art Institute of Chicago (4); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (2); Cleveland Museum of Art (1).

Questions

What is Berthe Morisot's most famous painting?

The Cradle (1872), at the Musée d'Orsay, showing her sister Edma watching over her sleeping baby.

Was Berthe Morisot related to Manet?

She married his younger brother, Eugène Manet, in 1874. Édouard Manet painted her several times, most famously in The Balcony and Berthe Morisot with a Bouquet of Violets.

How many Impressionist exhibitions did Morisot show in?

Seven of the eight. She missed only 1879, after the birth of her daughter Julie.
