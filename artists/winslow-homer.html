Winslow Homer: paintings, life and where to see them

Winslow Homer (1836–1910) was an American painter and printmaker, regarded as one of the greatest of the 19th century. He began as an illustrator of the Civil War and ended painting the sea at Prouts Neck, Maine.

Life

Homer was apprenticed to a Boston lithographer and became a freelance illustrator for Harper's Weekly, which sent him to the front in the Civil War. He was largely self-taught as a painter, and Prisoners from the Front (1866) made his reputation.

In the 1870s he painted American country life: schoolchildren, farms and boys at sea, as in Snap the Whip and Breezing Up. Two years at Cullercoats, an English fishing village, in 1881–82 turned him toward the sea and the people who work on it.

From 1883 he lived at Prouts Neck on the Maine coast, painting crashing surf and fishermen, and made brilliant watercolors in the Adirondacks, Florida and the Caribbean. He died at Prouts Neck in 1910.

Where to see Homer's work

The Met has The Gulf Stream and Prisoners from the Front, and the National Gallery of Art has Breezing Up. The Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, the Clark Art Institute and the Portland Museum of Art, which owns his studio at Prouts Neck, have major collections.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (11); Art Institute of Chicago (3); Smithsonian American Art Museum, Washington, DC (2); Cleveland Museum of Art (1); Hirshhorn Museum and Sculpture Garden, Washington, DC (1).

Questions

What is Winslow Homer's most famous painting?

The Gulf Stream (1899), at the Met, showing a sailor adrift in a dismasted boat surrounded by sharks. Breezing Up (A Fair Wind), at the National Gallery of Art, is also among his best known.

Did Winslow Homer paint the Civil War?

Yes. As an illustrator for Harper's Weekly he went to the front, and his paintings of soldiers, including Prisoners from the Front (1866), launched his career as a painter.

Where did Winslow Homer paint his seascapes?

At Prouts Neck, Maine, where he lived from 1883. His studio there is owned by the Portland Museum of Art and open for tours.
