Édouard Manet: paintings, life and where to see them

Édouard Manet (1832–1883) was a French painter of modern life whose scandalous Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe and Olympia opened the way for Impressionism. The Impressionists treated him as their leader, though he never exhibited with them.

Life

Manet was born into a wealthy Paris family, failed the naval academy's entrance exam, and trained for six years under the academic painter Thomas Couture. He admired Velázquez, Goya and Titian, and wanted success at the official Salon.

Instead he became its scandal. Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe was rejected in 1863 and shown at the Salon des Refusés, and Olympia caused an uproar in 1865. Younger painters gathered around him at the Café Guerbois, and his friend Berthe Morisot married his brother Eugène.

In the 1870s his palette lightened and he painted outdoors with Monet at Argenteuil. His last major painting was A Bar at the Folies-Bergère (1882). Ill for years, he died in 1883, at 51, soon after his left leg was amputated.

Where to see Manet's work

The Musée d'Orsay has Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe and Olympia, and the Courtauld Gallery in London has A Bar at the Folies-Bergère. In the US, the Met has more than a dozen of his paintings, and the National Gallery of Art and the Art Institute of Chicago have important ones.

Questions

What is the difference between Manet and Monet?

They were different painters, and friends. Édouard Manet (1832–1883) painted mostly people and scenes of Paris and never showed with the Impressionists; Claude Monet (1840–1926) painted mostly landscapes and water and was the Impressionists' central figure.

Was Manet an Impressionist?

Not formally. He never showed in the eight Impressionist exhibitions, but he was close to the group, painted with them in the 1870s, and is seen as their forerunner.

Why was Olympia so shocking?

Manet took the pose of Titian's Venus of Urbino and gave it to a Paris courtesan who stares straight back at the viewer, without the excuse of myth.
