Claude Monet: paintings, life and where to see them

Claude Monet (1840–1926) was the French painter at the center of Impressionism. His Impression, Sunrise gave the movement its name, and he spent a long life painting the same subjects in changing light, ending with the water lilies of his garden at Giverny.

Life

Monet grew up in Le Havre on the Normandy coast, where he first sold caricatures and met the landscape painter Eugène Boudin, who persuaded him to paint outdoors. In Paris in the 1860s he befriended Renoir, Sisley and Bazille, and spent years in poverty while the Salon rejected much of his work.

In 1874 he and his friends held their own exhibition, and a critic's mockery of Impression, Sunrise named the group. Through the 1870s he painted the river towns around Paris, especially Argenteuil. He settled at Giverny in 1883 and from 1890 painted series: stacks of wheat, poplars, Rouen Cathedral and, later, London's bridges and Parliament.

At Giverny he built a water garden with a Japanese footbridge, and from the late 1890s until his death he painted it about 250 times. Despite cataracts in his last years, he finished the great Water Lilies panels he gave to France, installed at the Musée de l'Orangerie in Paris in 1927, a few months after he died.

Where to see Monet's work

The Musée Marmottan Monet in Paris holds the largest collection of his work, and the Musée d'Orsay and the Orangerie have many of his best-known paintings. The Art Institute of Chicago has one of the largest Monet collections outside France, and the National Gallery of Art and the Met have strong holdings.

The paintings on this page: Art Institute of Chicago (11); National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (7); Cleveland Museum of Art (1); Musée Marmottan Monet, Paris (1).

Questions

What is Monet's most famous painting?

Impression, Sunrise (1872) matters most historically, since it named Impressionism. His Water Lilies, painted about 250 times at Giverny, are the best known.

How many water lilies did Monet paint?

About 250 paintings of the water lilies in his pond at Giverny, from the late 1890s until his death in 1926.

Can you visit Monet's garden?

Yes. His house and gardens at Giverny, about 75 kilometers (47 miles) northwest of Paris, are open to visitors from spring to autumn.
