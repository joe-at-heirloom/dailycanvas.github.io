Sandro Botticelli: paintings, life and where to see them

Sandro Botticelli (c. 1445–1510) was a Florentine painter of the Early Renaissance, known for The Birth of Venus and Primavera, the most famous mythological paintings of the age. His graceful, linear style was largely forgotten for three centuries before the Victorians rediscovered it.

Life

Born Alessandro di Mariano Filipepi, he took his nickname from his older brother, called botticello, "little barrel". He trained with Filippo Lippi and by the 1470s was working for the Medici and their circle.

In 1481–82 he was one of the painters called to Rome to decorate the walls of the Sistine Chapel. Back in Florence he painted Primavera and The Birth of Venus, along with many altarpieces and Madonnas.

In the 1490s, when the preacher Girolamo Savonarola dominated Florence, his work became more intensely religious. He died in Florence in 1510, out of fashion; his reputation revived only in the 19th century.

Where to see Botticelli's work

The Uffizi in Florence has The Birth of Venus, Primavera and many more, and his frescoes are on the walls of the Sistine Chapel. In the US, the National Gallery of Art in Washington has The Adoration of the Magi and several portraits, and the Met and the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston have fine works.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (5); Uffizi Galleries, Florence (2).

Questions

What is Botticelli's most famous painting?

The Birth of Venus (c. 1485), in the Uffizi in Florence. Primavera (c. 1480), in the same museum, is the other.

Who was the model for The Birth of Venus?

Legend says it was Simonetta Vespucci, a celebrated Florentine beauty, but there is no evidence for it. The face is an ideal rather than a portrait.

Where can I see Botticelli's paintings in the US?

The National Gallery of Art in Washington, the Met in New York and the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston.
