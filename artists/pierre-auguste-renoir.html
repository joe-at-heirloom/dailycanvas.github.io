Pierre-Auguste Renoir: paintings, life and where to see them

Pierre-Auguste Renoir (1841–1919) was a French Impressionist painter of people at their ease: dances, boating parties, families and friends. He was one of the movement's founders and among the most popular painters of any age.

Life

Renoir grew up in Paris and was apprenticed as a boy to paint porcelain. In 1862 he entered the studio of Charles Gleyre, where he met Monet, Sisley and Bazille, and in 1869 he and Monet painted side by side at La Grenouillère, a bathing place on the Seine.

He showed at the first Impressionist exhibition in 1874 and painted his best-known scenes of Parisian life, Bal du moulin de la Galette (1876) and Luncheon of the Boating Party (1880–81). After a trip to Italy in 1881–82 he turned for a time to a crisper, more classical style.

In his last decades rheumatoid arthritis crippled his hands, and he kept painting with a brush placed between his fingers, settling at Cagnes-sur-Mer in the south of France. His sons included the film director Jean Renoir.

Where to see Renoir's work

The Barnes Foundation in Philadelphia owns 181 of his paintings, the largest collection anywhere. The Musée d'Orsay has Bal du moulin de la Galette, the Phillips Collection in Washington has Luncheon of the Boating Party, and the National Gallery of Art, the Met and the Art Institute of Chicago have many more.

The paintings on this page: Art Institute of Chicago (8); National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (7); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (3); Cleveland Museum of Art (1); Phillips Collection, Washington, DC (1).

Questions

What is Renoir's most famous painting?

Bal du moulin de la Galette (1876), at the Musée d'Orsay, and Luncheon of the Boating Party (1880–81), at the Phillips Collection in Washington.

Did Renoir paint with arthritis?

Yes. Rheumatoid arthritis crippled his hands in his last decades, but he kept painting, with a brush placed between his fingers.

Where is the largest collection of Renoir paintings?

The Barnes Foundation in Philadelphia, with 181.
