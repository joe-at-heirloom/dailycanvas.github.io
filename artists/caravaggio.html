Caravaggio: paintings, life and where to see them

Caravaggio (1571–1610), born Michelangelo Merisi, was an Italian painter who changed European art with dramatic light, real-looking people and moments of high drama. His style was copied across Europe within a decade of his death.

Life

Caravaggio trained in Milan and arrived in Rome in the early 1590s, where he painted half-length figures, fruit and young musicians for collectors such as Cardinal Francesco del Monte. His first public commission, the paintings of Saint Matthew for the Contarelli Chapel in San Luigi dei Francesi (1599–1600), made him famous.

He painted saints and biblical stories with ordinary Romans as models, dirty feet and all, and lit them like a stage against deep darkness. Some patrons rejected his altarpieces as too coarse; others competed for his work.

His life was as violent as his pictures. In 1606 he killed a man in a brawl in Rome and fled with a death sentence over him, working in Naples, Malta and Sicily. He died in 1610 at Porto Ercole, on his way back to Rome to seek a pardon, at 38.

Where to see Caravaggio's work

Rome has the most: San Luigi dei Francesi, Santa Maria del Popolo, the Galleria Borghese and Palazzo Barberini. In the US, the Met has The Musicians and The Denial of Saint Peter, and there are single works at the Cleveland Museum of Art, the Kimbell Art Museum in Fort Worth, the Nelson-Atkins Museum in Kansas City and the Detroit Institute of Arts.

Questions

What is Caravaggio's most famous painting?

The Calling of Saint Matthew (1599–1600), still in the Contarelli Chapel in Rome. Judith Beheading Holofernes and Supper at Emmaus are also among his best known.

What is tenebrism?

Painting with very dark backgrounds and strong, focused light, so figures seem to step out of shadow. Caravaggio made it famous, and his followers, the Caravaggisti, spread it across Europe.

Where can I see Caravaggio in the US?

At the Met in New York (The Musicians and The Denial of Saint Peter), the Cleveland Museum of Art, the Kimbell Art Museum in Fort Worth, the Nelson-Atkins Museum in Kansas City and the Detroit Institute of Arts.
