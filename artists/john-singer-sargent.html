John Singer Sargent: paintings, life and where to see them

John Singer Sargent (1856–1925) was an American painter, born in Florence to American parents, who became the leading society portraitist of his era on both sides of the Atlantic. He was also a brilliant painter of watercolors and outdoor scenes.

Life

Sargent grew up traveling around Europe with his parents and trained in Paris under the portraitist Carolus-Duran. He made his name at the Paris Salon, but his portrait of Virginie Gautreau, Madame X, caused a scandal there in 1884.

He moved to London in 1886, where Carnation, Lily, Lily, Rose (1885–86) was a success, and became the portraitist of choice for British and American society, painting hundreds of commissioned portraits.

Around 1907 he largely gave up portraits for watercolors and murals, including decorations for the Boston Public Library and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. As an official war artist he painted Gassed (1919). He died in London in 1925.

Where to see Sargent's work

The Met has Madame X, and the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston has The Daughters of Edward Darley Boit and his murals. The Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum has El Jaleo, Tate Britain has Carnation, Lily, Lily, Rose, and the National Gallery of Art and the Smithsonian American Art Museum have many portraits.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (10); Art Institute of Chicago (3); Smithsonian American Art Museum, Washington, DC (2); Hirshhorn Museum and Sculpture Garden, Washington, DC (1).

Questions

What is John Singer Sargent's most famous painting?

Madame X (1883–84), at the Met. Its scandal at the 1884 Salon pushed him to move from Paris to London.

Was John Singer Sargent American?

Yes. He was born in Florence to American parents and lived mostly in Europe, but he kept his American citizenship all his life.

How many works did Sargent make?

About 900 oil paintings and more than 2,000 watercolors, plus many drawings.
