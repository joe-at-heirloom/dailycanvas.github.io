Gustav Klimt: paintings, life and where to see them

Gustav Klimt (1862–1918) was an Austrian painter and a founder of the Vienna Secession, best known for The Kiss and the gold-covered portraits of his golden period. He also painted quiet square landscapes on summer holidays at the Attersee.

Life

The son of a gold engraver, Klimt trained at Vienna's School of Applied Arts and began, with his brother Ernst and Franz Matsch, decorating theaters and museums, including ceilings at the Burgtheater and the stairwell of the Kunsthistorisches Museum.

In 1897 he led a group of artists out of the conservative establishment to found the Vienna Secession and became its first president. His paintings for the ceiling of the University of Vienna were attacked as obscene; they were later destroyed by fire at the end of the Second World War.

His golden period, about 1899 to 1910, produced the Beethoven Frieze, Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I and The Kiss, made with real gold leaf. He died in Vienna in February 1918 after a stroke, weakened by the influenza epidemic, at 55.

Where to see Klimt's work

The Belvedere in Vienna has the largest collection, including The Kiss; the Leopold Museum and the Wien Museum have more, and the Beethoven Frieze is in the Secession building. In New York, the Neue Galerie has Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I, MoMA has Hope II, and the Met has Mäda Primavesi.

Questions

What is Klimt's most famous painting?

The Kiss (1907–08), in the Belvedere in Vienna. Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I (1907), now at the Neue Galerie in New York, is the other.

Did Klimt use real gold?

Yes. In his golden period he laid gold and silver leaf into the paint, drawing on his father's trade as a gold engraver and on the Byzantine mosaics he saw in Ravenna.

Where can I see The Kiss?

At the Upper Belvedere in Vienna. The Austrian state bought it in 1908, the year it was first shown.
