Katsushika Hokusai: prints, life and where to see them

Katsushika Hokusai (1760–1849) was a Japanese painter and printmaker, and the artist of The Great Wave off Kanagawa, probably the most recognizable work of Asian art in the world. He worked for some seventy years and was still experimenting in his late eighties.

Life

Hokusai trained as a woodblock carver and then in the studio of the print artist Katsukawa Shunshō, making prints of actors and courtesans in the ukiyo-e tradition. He changed his art name more than thirty times over his career.

From 1814 he published the Hokusai Manga, volumes of sketches of people, animals and nature that circulated widely. Around 1830–32, at about seventy, he produced Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, the series that includes The Great Wave and Red Fuji, using the newly imported Prussian blue. It was so popular that ten more prints were added.

He kept working until his death in Edo in 1849, at 88. His prints reached Europe after Japan opened to trade in the 1850s and influenced Monet, Van Gogh, Degas and the designers of Art Nouveau.

Where to see Hokusai's work

The Great Wave was printed thousands of times, so many museums own impressions: the Met, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, the Art Institute of Chicago, the British Museum and the Sumida Hokusai Museum in Tokyo among them. Prints fade in light and are shown only for short periods, so check before you go.

The prints on this page: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (19); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (one of many impressions) (1).

Questions

Is The Great Wave a painting?

No. It's a color woodblock print from the series Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, made around 1830–32 and printed in large numbers, which is why many museums own one.

What is Hokusai's most famous work?

The Great Wave off Kanagawa. South Wind, Clear Sky, known as Red Fuji, from the same series, is a close second.

How old was Hokusai when he made The Great Wave?

About seventy. He began Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji around 1830 and kept working for nearly twenty more years.
