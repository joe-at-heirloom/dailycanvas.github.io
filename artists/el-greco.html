El Greco: paintings, life and where to see them

El Greco (1541–1614), "the Greek", was a painter born on Crete who made his career in Toledo, Spain. His elongated, flickering figures and visionary color looked strange to his own century and astonishingly modern to the 20th.

Life

Born Doménikos Theotokópoulos in Venetian-ruled Crete, he trained as an icon painter, then moved to Venice around 1567, where he absorbed the color of Titian and Tintoretto, and to Rome in 1570.

In 1577 he settled in Toledo, the religious capital of Spain, and stayed for the rest of his life. His masterpiece, The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (1586–88), still hangs in the church of Santo Tomé there. He painted altarpieces, saints, portraits and the stormy View of Toledo.

After his death in 1614 he was largely forgotten. Rediscovered in the late 19th century, he became a hero to modern painters, from Cézanne and Picasso to the German Expressionists.

Where to see El Greco's work

Toledo has the most: Santo Tomé, the cathedral and the El Greco Museum. The Prado in Madrid has many more. In the US, the Met has View of Toledo and Cardinal Fernando Niño de Guevara, and the National Gallery of Art, the Art Institute of Chicago and the Cleveland Museum of Art have important works.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (4); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (2); Cleveland Museum of Art (1).

Questions

What is El Greco's most famous painting?

The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (1586–88), in the church of Santo Tomé in Toledo, and View of Toledo (c. 1599–1600), at the Met.

Why is he called El Greco?

It means "the Greek" in Spanish. He was born Doménikos Theotokópoulos on Crete and signed his paintings in Greek letters.

Why are El Greco's figures so elongated?

It's a deliberate style, rooted in Mannerism and Byzantine icons, that makes figures seem spiritual and weightless. The old theory that he had astigmatism has been discredited.
