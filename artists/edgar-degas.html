Edgar Degas: paintings, life and where to see them

Edgar Degas (1834–1917) was a French painter and sculptor who exhibited with the Impressionists but preferred to call himself a realist. He is best known for his ballet dancers, seen backstage and in rehearsal from unusual angles.

Life

Degas came from a wealthy banking family, studied at the École des Beaux-Arts and admired Ingres above all. He began with history paintings and portraits, and in 1872–73 visited his mother's family in New Orleans, where he painted A Cotton Office in New Orleans.

He helped organize the Impressionist exhibitions and showed in seven of the eight, but he worked in the studio, from drawings and memory, rather than outdoors. His subjects were modern Paris: dancers, cafés, laundresses, milliners and the racetrack.

He worked more and more in pastel as his eyesight failed, and modeled sculptures in wax, including Little Dancer Aged Fourteen, shown in 1881. He never married and died in Paris in 1917.

Where to see Degas's work

The Musée d'Orsay has the largest collection. In the US, the Met, the National Gallery of Art, the Art Institute of Chicago and the Norton Simon Museum in Pasadena all have major holdings, including bronze casts of his sculptures.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (6); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (4); Art Institute of Chicago (2).

Questions

Why did Degas paint so many dancers?

The Paris Opéra ballet let him study bodies in movement and the backstage life of modern Paris. Dancers were his most frequent subject from the 1870s on.

Was Degas an Impressionist?

He showed in seven of the eight Impressionist exhibitions, but he rejected the label, preferred the studio to painting outdoors, and called himself a realist.

What is Degas's most famous work?

Little Dancer Aged Fourteen, the only sculpture he exhibited in his lifetime, and his paintings and pastels of the ballet, such as The Dance Class.
