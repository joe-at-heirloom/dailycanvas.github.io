Francisco Goya: paintings, life and where to see them

Francisco Goya (1746–1828) was a Spanish painter and printmaker, court painter to the Spanish crown and a fierce witness to war. He is often called the last of the Old Masters and the first of the moderns.

Life

Goya grew up in Aragon and made his name in Madrid designing cartoons for the royal tapestry factory. He rose to become First Court Painter to Charles IV in 1799, painting the royal family, the aristocracy and the famous pair The Clothed Maja and The Nude Maja.

A severe illness in 1792–93 left him deaf, and his work turned darker. He published the satirical prints Los Caprichos in 1799 and, after the French invasion of Spain in 1808, recorded its atrocities in the prints The Disasters of War and in The Second of May 1808 and The Third of May 1808 (1814).

In his seventies he painted the Black Paintings, including Saturn Devouring His Son, directly onto the walls of his house outside Madrid; they were later moved to canvas and are in the Prado. In 1824 he left for Bordeaux, where he died in 1828.

Where to see Goya's work

The Museo del Prado in Madrid holds the largest collection, including the Black Paintings and both Majas. In the US, the Met, the National Gallery of Art and the Frick Collection have important portraits.

The paintings on this page: Museo del Prado, Madrid (2); National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (2); The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York (2).

Questions

What is Goya's most famous painting?

The Third of May 1808 (1814), in the Prado, showing French soldiers executing Spanish civilians. Saturn Devouring His Son, one of the Black Paintings, is the other image most people know.

What are Goya's Black Paintings?

Fourteen dark, nightmarish scenes Goya painted on the walls of his house near Madrid in 1819–23, never meant for display. They were transferred to canvas after his death and are now in the Prado.

Was Goya deaf?

Yes. An illness in 1792–93 left him permanently deaf in his mid-forties, and his later work grew darker and more personal.
