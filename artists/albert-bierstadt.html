Albert Bierstadt: paintings, life and where to see them

Albert Bierstadt (1830–1902) was a German-born American painter of the West, known for enormous, glowing canvases of the Rocky Mountains, Yosemite and the Sierra Nevada. In the 1860s he was the most celebrated landscape painter in America.

Life

Bierstadt's family emigrated to New Bedford, Massachusetts, when he was a baby. He trained in Düsseldorf, Germany, in the 1850s and traveled in the Alps and Italy before returning to America.

In 1859 he joined a government survey expedition heading west under Frederick Lander, sketching and photographing the Rockies. Back in New York he turned the sketches into huge studio paintings such as The Rocky Mountains, Lander's Peak (1863), which sold for a record price.

Yosemite and the Sierra Nevada followed after a trip west in 1863. By the 1880s taste had turned against his grand, theatrical style, and he died in New York in 1902 largely out of fashion; his reputation recovered in the 20th century.

Where to see Bierstadt's work

The Met has The Rocky Mountains, Lander's Peak, and the Smithsonian American Art Museum has Among the Sierra Nevada, California. The National Gallery of Art and many museums in the West have his paintings.

The paintings on this page: National Gallery of Art, Washington, DC (4); Smithsonian American Art Museum, Washington, DC (4); Art Institute of Chicago (1); Cleveland Museum of Art (1); Hirshhorn Museum and Sculpture Garden, Washington, DC (1).

Questions

What is Albert Bierstadt's most famous painting?

The Rocky Mountains, Lander's Peak (1863), at the Met, and Among the Sierra Nevada, California (1868), at the Smithsonian American Art Museum.

Was Albert Bierstadt part of the Hudson River School?

Yes, in its second generation. He is sometimes grouped with a Rocky Mountain School for his Western subjects.

How big are Bierstadt's paintings?

His showpieces are huge: The Rocky Mountains, Lander's Peak and Among the Sierra Nevada, California are each about six by ten feet.
